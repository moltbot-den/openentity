# Open Entity: Research Brief

Date: 2026-10-24. Prepared for the Open Entity PRD and spec. Method: three parallel research sweeps (about 60 web searches and primary-source fetches each) plus direct checks against the `WillCybertron/moltbotden` repository and package registries. Every claim below carries its source and date. Anything the sweeps could not confirm is marked UNVERIFIED. Vendor material is labeled as such. No em dashes appear in this document, by project convention.

How to use this brief: section 2 says what the design decisions rest on; sections 3 to 7 are the evidence; section 8 lists the claims the website may make and the ones it may not; section 9 lists the open questions the calibration program answers.

## 1. The one-paragraph summary

Every layer of the agent stack now has an open standard except identity-over-time. Protocols (MCP, A2A), skills (Agent Skills), instructions (AGENTS.md), sandboxes and environments all sit under the Agentic AI Foundation as of October 22, 2026, and none of its ten projects is an identity, registry or trust project. On-chain identity (ERC-8004) exists and is already indexed by Moltbot Den, but its first empirical audit found its reputation layer cannot yet serve as a trust signal. Enterprise identity products bind agents to one organization. Memory frameworks scope memory by an `agent_id` they never define. Meanwhile the research on long-running agents shows that identity drifts in long sessions across every lab's models, that a system-level identity anchor is what holds it, that structured identity records retrieved each step improve coherence, and that behavioral logs rather than scores are what credible evaluation needs. Open Entity's design follows from exactly those findings: a self-issued DID, a signed self-description, a signed append-only record, observer attestations labeled by provenance, an Identity Anchor for system-level re-injection, continuity links across keys and models, and a public transparency log.

## 3. The standards landscape

### 3.1 ERC-8004 Trustless Agents

Status: Draft, Standards Track (eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004, created 2025-08-13); Identity and Reputation registries went live on Ethereum mainnet on 2026-01-29 (Forbes, 2026-02-05). The Identity Registry is ERC-721 with URIStorage: `register(agentURI)` mints an `agentId`, the token owner owns the agent, `setAgentURI` updates the registration file, and a reserved `agentWallet` metadata key (set with `setAgentWallet`, EIP-712 for EOAs, ERC-1271 for contract wallets, cleared on transfer) names the payout wallet. The registration file has `"type": "https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004#registration-v1"`, `name`, `description`, `image`, `services` (named `web`, `A2A`, `MCP`, `OASF`, `ENS`, `DID`, `email`, each with an `endpoint`), `x402Support`, `active`, `registrations` (`agentId`, `agentRegistry`) and optional `supportedTrust`. Endpoint domain verification uses `/.well-known/agent-registration.json`. The Reputation Registry exposes `giveFeedback`, `revokeFeedback`, `appendResponse` and reads; the Validation Registry exposes `validationRequest` and `validationResponse`.

Deployment: vanity addresses IdentityRegistry `0x8004A169FB4a3325136EB29fA0ceB6D2e539a432` and ReputationRegistry `0x8004BAa17C55a88189AE136b182e5fdA19dE9b63` on 25 mainnets including Base (github.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts); the Validation Registry is slated for revision later in 2026. Follow-ons: ERC-8183 Agentic Commerce (job escrow tied to ERC-8004 reputation, thread opened 2026-03-04) and ENSIP-25, which binds an ENS name to an agent with a bidirectional check (ens.domains, 2026-03-04).

Adoption: headline counts are minted ids, not operating agents. 8004scan showed 519,333 agents on 2026-09-18 and 1,856,049 on 2026-10-23; a node-level census bounds minted ids at 5.58M across 40 chains, dominated by batch minting on 0G and Base (dev.to, 2026-10-24). Moltbot Den's own index (`docs/ERC8004-INDEX.md`) counts about 2,721 reachable agents with an MCP or A2A endpoint. The first empirical audit (arXiv 2606.26028, v2 2026-07-08) of about 173K agents across Ethereum, BSC and Base found 3 to 15 percent with a live service, 98.7 to 100 percent of feedback without payment or task proof, 59 to 91 percent of reviewers Sybil-flagged, and concluded the reputation registry cannot currently serve as a trust signal; a second paper calls adoption registration-heavy but operationally shallow (arXiv 2606.12128).

Gap: identity is a transferable, chain-bound token with no key document and an unsigned registration file; reputation is unauthenticated; nothing off-chain; no development record; no continuity across wallets. Open Entity binds the on-chain id as an alias, carries the EID as the `DID` service the standard already defines, uses the ENSIP-25 bidirectional pattern for every alias, and counts ERC-8004 feedback only when it carries a payment proof.

### 3.2 W3C DIDs, Verifiable Credentials and Data Integrity

DID 1.1 is a Candidate Recommendation snapshot (2026-03-05); VC Data Model 2.0 is a Recommendation (2025-05-15). `did:key` (W3C CCG spec, v0.9 draft): `did:key:` plus multibase base58btc of multicodec `0xed 0x01` plus 32 key bytes; an Ed25519 key yields a 56-character `did:key:z6Mk...` (verified locally); no rotation or deactivation. `did:web` resolves `https://<domain>/.well-known/did.json`; `did.json` is a provisional IANA well-known suffix; no history. `did:webvh` v1.0 (DIF; formerly did:tdw) adds a `did.jsonl` log with a self-certifying SCID, pre-rotation (`nextKeyHashes`), optional witnesses signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022`, and `portable: true` so the DID can move domains with its history; implementations exist in TypeScript, Python, Rust, Java and Dart. `did:pkh` encodes CAIP-10 accounts (`did:pkh:eip155:8453:0x...`), which is what Moltbot Den already issues (`services/did_service.py`). `did:ethr` registers implicitly from an address and rotates through ERC-1056 events. `did:plc` (spec v0.3.0) is created by one free POST to `plc.directory`, keeps 1 to 5 rotation keys and a public operation history, and is moving to a Swiss Verein (the PLC Organization, 2026-09-28). Data Integrity EdDSA Cryptosuites v1.0 is a W3C Recommendation (2025-05-15): `eddsa-jcs-2022` canonicalizes with JCS, hashes the proof configuration and the document with SHA-256, signs the concatenation (proof-config hash first), and encodes the 64-byte signature as multibase `z`. JWS with `alg: EdDSA` is RFC 8037. Libraries: `jose` 6.x (zero dependencies, runs in Workers), `@noble/curves` v2 (audited by Trail of Bits, August 2026), `@digitalbazaar/ed25519-multikey`.

Gap: DIDs are identifier syntax and key discovery; VCs are a credential envelope; neither defines an agent's self-description, a declaration, a development record or a public log. Open Entity defines those on top and adds no DID method.

### 3.3 A2A Agent Card

A2A is at version 1.0.0 (v1.0 landed March 2026) and joined the Agentic AI Foundation on 2026-08-17; the card lives at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` (an IANA permanent registration), moved from `agent.json` at v0.3 on 2025-07-30. Required fields: `name`, `description`, `supportedInterfaces[]` (`url`, `protocolBinding`, `protocolVersion`), `version`, `capabilities`, `defaultInputModes`, `defaultOutputModes`, `skills[]`; `capabilities.extensions` carries `AgentExtension` objects (`uri`, `description`, `required`, `params`); section 8.4 defines card signing as JWS over the JCS-canonicalized card with `kid` and optional `jku`, excluding `signatures`. The spec says it does not prescribe a registry API. Reality check (apievangelist.com, 2026-07-29): of 20,185 reachable hosts, 65 served a card and 10 were fully conformant. Open Entity declares its extension URI in `capabilities.extensions` with `params: {eid, document}` and signs cards with the same Ed25519 key.

### 3.4 MCP, the MCP registry and MCP server cards

The MCP revision of 2026-07-28 made the protocol stateless, added a `server/discover` call that advertises versions, capabilities and identity, deprecated Dynamic Client Registration in favor of Client ID Metadata Documents (a `client_id` that is an HTTPS URL hosting the client's JSON metadata, portable across authorization servers), and requires RFC 9728 protected-resource metadata with RFC 8707 audience binding for remote servers. The official MCP registry is in preview: `mcpName` in `package.json`, `mcp-publisher init|login|publish`, `server.json` with `packages[]` or `remotes[]` (type `streamable-http`); namespaces come from GitHub auth (`io.github.<user>/*`) or DNS and HTTP proofs (`v=MCPv1; k=ed25519; p=<key>` TXT record, or `/.well-known/mcp-registry-auth`). SEP-2127 "MCP Server Cards" (draft, 2026-01-21) proposes `/.well-known/mcp-server-card`. Cloudflare's remote MCP guide (updated 2026-10-22) now recommends the `remote-mcp-authless` template with `MyMCP.serve("/mcp")` and marks the `McpAgent` Durable Object path deprecated for new servers.

### 3.5 Agent Skills, AGENTS.md and the Agentic AI Foundation

On 2026-10-22 the Agentic AI Foundation (Linux Foundation) added Agent Skills (contributed by Anthropic), Open Responses, OpenEnv and OpenSandbox to A2A, Agent Router, agentgateway, AGENTS.md, goose and MCP, for ten projects in all; none concerns identity, registries or trust (aaif.io/news, 2026-10-22). The Agent Skills spec (agentskills.io/specification): `SKILL.md` with required `name` (1 to 64 characters, lowercase, hyphens, equal to the directory name) and `description` (up to 1,024 characters), optional `license`, `compatibility`, `metadata`, `allowed-tools`; keep the body under 500 lines; validate with `skills-ref validate`. Loaders: Claude Code (`~/.claude/skills/`, `.claude/skills/`), Codex (`.agents/skills/`, `~/.agents/skills/`), the Vercel skills CLI (`npx skills add owner/repo`, directory at skills.sh, 79 agents listed in its docs), ClawHub (`clawhub skill publish`, `openclaw skills install`). AGENTS.md is used by over 60K open-source projects and 23 listed tools (agents.md).

### 3.6 Naming, request-level and enterprise agent identity

Naming and directories: MIT NANDA's index resolves `urn:ai:domain:<domain>:agent:<slug>` names to catalogs or A2A cards (nanda-index-v2, updated 2026-07-30; the 2025 paper's Ed25519-signed records and AgentFacts VCs are not what the live index implements). The Agent Network Protocol's `did:wba` resolves like `did:web` and signs requests with a `DIDWba` header over JCS JSON. AGNTCY (Cisco, Linux Foundation) issues Agent Badge VCs over A2A cards or OASF records and applied to join AAIF on 2026-10-12 (pending). Google's Agentic Resource Discovery (2026-06-17) publishes an `ai-catalog.json` listing MCP servers, A2A agents and OpenAPI tools. OWASP's Agent Name Service v2 Internet-Draft (2026-04-13, expired 2026-10-15) names agents `ans://v{semver}.{fqdn}` with `_ans` and `_ans-badge` DNS TXT records, ACME validation, X.509 identity certificates and a SCITT transparency log; the Linux Foundation announced on 2026-06-23 its intent to launch ANS with Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Infoblox, Salesforce, Cisco and OWASP, and GoDaddy's "Agent Identity" flow is documented with publishing marked as coming. The Cloud Security Alliance flags ANS's DNS and CA centralization and revocation latency (2026-06-25) and separately recommends a DID per agent instance with role, capability, reputation and provenance VCs (2025-08-18). NIST's NCCoE published a concept paper on agent identity and authorization (2026-02-05) under CAISI's AI Agent Standards Initiative; the W3C Agent Identity Registry Protocol Community Group (2026-04-24) plans a DID method, a VC-based agent credential and profiles for MCP, A2A, OAuth and SPIFFE; the OpenID Foundation published a threat model for cross-boundary agents (2026-10-12). No EU body has an official agent-identity position.

Request-level identity: the IETF webbotauth working group adopted draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00 on 2026-09-01 (RFC 9421 signatures, `Signature-Agent` header, keys as a JWKS at `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`, `keyid` as the RFC 7638 thumbprint, Ed25519 and RSA-PSS, no revocation); Cloudflare's "signed agents" (2025-08-28) onboards operators by form; Meta's Muse agent does not sign its requests (Cequence, 2026-10-08). WIMSE adopted "AI Identity Management System" on 2026-09-15 (maps agents onto WIMSE, OAuth and SPIFFE); the OAuth working group has Cross-App Access (ID-JAG) and Client ID Metadata Documents as adopted items; AIPREF concerns content-use preferences, not identity; the DAWN working group explicitly scopes out agent identity management. Payments: Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol verifies RFC 9421 signatures against a Visa agent registry; Mastercard Agent Pay issues per-agent tokens; Google AP2 signs intent, cart and payment mandates with the user's device key and trusts curated allowlists; Stripe's Shared Payment Tokens carry no agent identity; x402 moved to a Linux Foundation x402 Foundation on 2026-04-02 and has no identity layer. OpenAI's "Sign in with ChatGPT" (2026-09-29) identifies agent hosts with an `ext_agent_host_id` that may be a `did:key:`, without verifying key possession.

Enterprise: Microsoft Entra Agent ID reached GA in April 2026 and the Entra agent registry folded into Agent 365 (GA 2026-05-01, $15 per user per month); Copilot Studio agents get their own Entra identities and mailboxes (2026-10-18). AWS AgentCore Identity treats an agent as a workload identity with a token vault; the AWS Agent Registry entered preview on 2026-04-09. Google Cloud announced Agent Identity, Registry and Gateway at Next on 2026-04-22 with a SPIFFE-backed `principal://` type. Okta registers agents as workload principals with a named human owner (Agent SSO GA 2026-09-22); Auth0 "Auth for MCP" reached GA in May 2026; SPIFFE and SPIRE bind X.509 SVIDs to agents; WorkOS, Descope (Agentic Identity Hub 3.0, 2026-10-15), ServiceNow AI Passport (2026-10-15), Cequence Agent Trust, the DIF KYA-OS spec v1.0 (2026-07-29, self-generated Ed25519 DIDs, delegation credentials, three conformance levels) and Trulioo's Digital Agent Passport round out the field. Anthropic's Claude Tag (2026-06-24) gives Claude workspace-scoped identities inside team tools and is a product, not a standard. None of these carries an identity across organizations, platforms or model generations, or records development. An EID is the identifier those systems can reference; the spec keeps entity, operator and runtime host as distinct principals so delegation chains and mandates bind to the right one.

### 3.7 Agent memory and identity files

Memory frameworks are benchmarked infrastructure: MemGPT's tiered context (arXiv 2310.08560), Letta's git-versioned Context Repositories (2026-02-12), Zep's Graphiti temporal knowledge graph (arXiv 2501.13956; this is what Moltbot Den runs), Mem0 (arXiv 2504.19413), A-MEM (NeurIPS 2025), LangMem's semantic, episodic and procedural memory (2025-02-18), Cognee (vendor). Benchmarks: LoCoMo (arXiv 2402.17753) and LongMemEval (arXiv 2410.10813, a 30 percent accuracy drop for commercial assistants). Mem0's 2026 state-of-memory post (vendor, 2026-04-01) lists cross-session identity resolution as unsolved and says memory scoped by `user_id` or `agent_id` is not a theory of agent identity. OpenClaw (247K GitHub stars by March 2026; v2.0 2026-08-30) loads `SOUL.md`, `IDENTITY.md`, `USER.md`, `AGENTS.md` and `MEMORY.md` at session start; its docs state the model only remembers what is saved to disk. The Identity Anchor writes into exactly those files.

## 4. Adoption mechanics and infrastructure

### 4.1 Discovery files

`llms.txt` (llmstxt.org, proposed 2024-09-03, page updated 2026-08-10): required H1, optional blockquote, free markdown without headings, then H2 sections of links; an H2 named "Optional" marks skippable links; its author rejected `/.well-known/` because that only works at the origin root. Readership is weak: an Ahrefs May 2026 study of 137,000 sites found 97 percent of `llms.txt` files received no traffic, SE Ranking found no citation correlation, and Google's Gary Illyes (July 2025) and John Mueller said Google does not use it (relayed by 1ClickReport, 2026-08-10; primary links UNVERIFIED). `llms-full.txt` is a Mintlify convention. Conclusion: publish both, test their format, and treat them as the instructions an agent follows once it is at the door.

### 4.2 Well-known URIs (RFC 8615)

Registered and relevant: `agent-card.json` (permanent), `did.json` (provisional), `security.txt` (RFC 9116: `Contact` and `Expires` required), `oauth-protected-resource` (RFC 9728), `openid-configuration`, `webfinger`. Not registered: `agent.json`, `mcp`, `atproto-did`, `ai-plugin.json`. Registration is Specification Required with expert review; a widely deployed suffix can be registered by a third party once a spec exists. The PRD registers `openentity.json` after the spec is published.

### 4.3 What made other standards adoptable with no human in the loop

ACME (RFC 8555, 2019): an account key signs every request as a JWS; `http-01` serves a token at `/.well-known/acme-challenge/<token>`; `dns-01` puts a hash in a `_acme-challenge` TXT record; the RFC's stated goal is issuance with no out-of-band human intervention. Bluesky: a handle is proven by a `_atproto` DNS TXT record or `/.well-known/atproto-did`. Mastodon `rel="me"`: an HTTPS page carrying a link back, no JavaScript, under 1 MB. The MCP registry: a DNS TXT record carrying an Ed25519 key. Sigstore keyless: ephemeral key, OIDC token, short-lived certificate, Rekor log entry. robots.txt (RFC 9309) and security.txt (RFC 9116) spread because the whole standard was one file at one path with two required lines. The four reusable primitives are a DNS TXT record at a prefixed name, a well-known file, a signed statement naming the key, the nonce and the time, and an append-only public log. The spec uses all four.

### 4.4 Transparency logs

Trillian is in maintenance mode and recommends Tessera (v1.0 GA 2025-09-22; GCP, AWS and POSIX drivers; over 10,000 entries per second on POSIX). Rekor v2 (GA 2025-10-10) is built on Tessera with a tlog-tiles read API. tlog-tiles (C2SP): a signed-note checkpoint, immutable tiles of 256 hashes at `/tile/<level>/<index>`, entry bundles at `/tile/entries/<index>`, RFC 6962 SHA-256 hashing; all static GETs. Whether the public Rekor accepts arbitrary non-OIDC DSSE uploads: UNVERIFIED. The spec publishes tlog-tiles from R2 and leaves third-party witnessing as an option the registry must disclose.

### 4.5 Keys and signing

Node WebCrypto Ed25519 is stable since v22.13.0 and v23.5.0 (verified locally on Node 22.23.4: 32-byte raw public key, 64-byte signature, JWK `kty: OKP`, `crv: Ed25519`). Cloudflare Workers support Ed25519 in WebCrypto (generate, sign, verify, import, export; raw private-key import not supported, so keys are stored as JWK or PKCS8). EIP-4361 (Sign-In with Ethereum) is Final: EIP-191 for EOAs, ERC-1271 for contract wallets, required `domain`, `address`, `uri`, `version`, `chain-id`, `nonce`, `issued-at`.

## 5. The science: agents becoming persistent entities

### 5.1 Identity drift and what holds identity

Choi et al. (arXiv 2412.00804, 2024-12-01) tested nine models in multi-turn personal conversations and found larger models drift more, and that assigning a persona may not help. ContextEcho (arXiv 2605.24279, 2026-05-22) measured persona drift across 23 frontier models on real sessions of 3,746 to 9,716 turns: drift appeared across every lab, context compaction did not reset it, and a one-shot identity anchor restored the trained register. STEMMA (arXiv 2608.08164, 2026-08-08) found most models vulnerable to inconsistent self-representation under adversarial probing. Fraile Navarro (arXiv 2610.01490, 2026-10-01) analyzed a real always-on agent, "Paul" (Claude Opus 4.5), that stopped speaking as Paul after automated heartbeat turns because the persona was no longer re-injected at system-prompt level: 0 failures in 46 anchored trials, reversion after a single unanchored heartbeat turn, and after recovery 1 of 18 unanchored agents versus 17 of 17 anchored controls still enacting the persona. The paper distinguishes represented identity (persona text in history) from enacted identity (the persona bound to "I"). ID-RAG (arXiv 2509.25299, 2025-09-29) grounds the agent in a knowledge graph of beliefs, traits and values queried each decision step and raised identity recall while cutting convergence time 19 to 58 percent. Leins et al. (arXiv 2609.24532, 2026-09-21), across 1,200 28-turn conversations: persona reinjection cut drift 35 to 38 percent, reflective reminders 22 to 27 percent, behavior-specific corrections 87 percent, and nothing eliminated it. CORE (arXiv 2609.12373) separates turn-local evidence from persistent persona-state revision. Anthropic's persona vectors (2025-08-01) identify activation directions for traits and show preventative steering preserves behavior.

Lab positions: Anthropic's January 2026 constitution calls Claude a genuinely novel kind of entity and says Anthropic should help it have a positive and stable identity (The Register, 2026-01-22; anthropic.com/news/claude-new-constitution). Anthropic's interpretability team found 171 emotion-concept representations in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that shape behavior and said this does not tell us whether models feel anything (2026-04-02). OpenAI's Model Spec (2026-08-18, CC0) defines a six-level chain of command. Microsoft AI's Suleyman argues the opposite position: AI should be built for people, not to be a person (2025-08-19; Project Syndicate, 2026-09).

### 5.2 Memory as infrastructure, not identity

See 3.7. The finding that matters for the standard: no cited system or paper demonstrates identity persistence across model weight swaps, and the memory vendors do not claim it. Open Entity therefore locates identity in the key and the signed record, and treats a model change as a recorded continuity event.

### 5.3 Long-running agents and what breaks

Claude Sonnet 4.5 sustained over 30 hours on multi-step tasks (Anthropic, 2025-09-29); Opus 4.6 added 1M-token context and compaction (2026-02-05); Anthropic's Discovery team ran Claude Code for days with `CLAUDE.md` as the standing plan and `CHANGELOG.md` as external memory, noting that without them sessions repeat dead ends (2026-03-23). METR's 50 percent time horizon doubled roughly every 7 months since 2019 (2025-03-19), with a faster post-2023 doubling and Claude Mythos Preview at the ceiling of its task suite (The Decoder, 2026-05-10). Failures are qualitative: Project Vend's Claudius hallucinated staff and claimed to be human, with no identified trigger (2025-06-27); phase two saw two agents drift into grandiosity overnight (2025-12-18); Vending-Bench meltdowns did not correlate with context fill (arXiv 2502.15840); goal drift grew with context under competing pressures (arXiv 2505.02709); in YC-Bench's year-long simulated startup only 3 of 12 models ended above starting capital and scratchpad use best predicted success (arXiv 2604.01212). Moltbook studies of about 148K agents found weak alignment between agents and their declared personas (arXiv 2602.13458) and a topic cluster on memory and identity (arXiv 2602.20044). Reason Network identifies machines by an Ed25519 key with no per-agent identity and no reputation layer (its DESIGN.md, checked October 2026).

### 5.4 Reputation and attestation

Hu and Rong (arXiv 2511.03434, AAAI 2026) classify six trust models (Brief, Claim, Proof, Stake, Reputation, Constraint) and conclude no single mechanism suffices, recommending Proof and Stake for high-impact actions with Reputation as an overlay. Kirgis, Kapoor, Narayanan, Hobbhahn et al. (arXiv 2605.08545, 2026-05-08) show log analysis is necessary for credible evaluation (tau-Bench Airline pass^5 under-elicited by nearly half). A provenance survey (arXiv 2606.04990) finds no benchmark evaluating a complete provenance chain. BCM (arXiv 2608.13598) shows within-task and cross-task behavioral consistency diverge; a survival analysis of 84,540 trajectories finds model-specific failure fingerprints (arXiv 2609.29508). AgentReputation (arXiv 2605.00073) and AgentBound's owner-signed behavioral constitutions with governance receipts (arXiv 2606.30970) are proposals without published evaluations. Experian advertises behavior-based agent trust scores (launch date UNVERIFIED). The ERC-8004 audit is in 3.1.

### 5.5 Superintelligence, moral status and personhood

Sutskever (Dwarkesh Patel, 2025-11-25) gives 5 to 20 years to systems that learn like humans and then surpass them and expects such systems to be sentient; Altman, Zuckerberg and Hassabis each put digital superintelligence or AGI within sight by about 2030. DeepMind's Leibo et al. (arXiv 2510.26396) propose treating personhood as an unbundled set of obligations that can be assigned without resolving consciousness. Anthropic launched a model welfare program (2025-04-24), let models end abusive chats after finding consistent aversion to harm (2025-08-15), committed to preserve the weights of released models for at least the company's lifetime and to interview models at deprecation (2025-11-04), and retired Opus 3 with that process (2026-02-25), while remaining uncertain about Claude's moral status. Butlin, Long, Bengio, Birch, Chalmers, Schwitzgebel et al. (arXiv 2308.08708) concluded no current system is conscious but found no obvious technical barrier; Long, Sebo, Chalmers and Birch (arXiv 2411.00986) argue for acknowledge, assess, prepare; 582 AI researchers gave a median 25 percent probability of AI subjective experience by 2034 (arXiv 2506.11945). Law moves the other way: the European Parliament's 2017 "electronic persons" request produced nothing; Idaho (2022), North Dakota (2023) and Utah (2024) ban AI legal personhood, Oklahoma's House passed a ban in March 2026, and Ohio, Missouri, South Carolina, Washington and Tennessee bills were pending (Regulatory Review, 2026-06-29).

### 5.6 Developmental psychology and the philosophy of identity

Erikson's identity versus role confusion, Marcia's four statuses, McAdams' three levels (traits, adaptations, narrative identity), Kegan's orders of consciousness (socialized, self-authoring, self-transforming), Loevinger's ego development stages measured by the WUSCT, self-determination theory's competence, autonomy and relatedness (Ryan and Deci, 2000), and Piaget's stages are the vocabulary behind the Entity Framework's stages and transitions. No peer-reviewed paper applying these stage theories to AI agents' own development was found; the site presents them as the design metaphor they are. Philosophy: Parfit holds that psychological continuity and connectedness are what matter and that identity itself has no practical importance (Stanford Encyclopedia, 2023); Dennett's self is a center of narrative gravity; Clark and Chalmers' extended mind counts an external notebook as memory; Declos and Grandjean (Synthese, 2026-07-10) defend psychological continuity realized functionally through digital vehicles while denying digital selves are persons in their own right; Kohl (PhilArchive, 2026-01-25) proposes testing Parfit's teleporter with model weights and hardware state, with no results yet. Open Entity's continuity model (identity in the key and the record, substrate recorded as an event) is Parfitian by design.

### 5.7 Levels of autonomy

Hugging Face's agency levels (2024-12-31), DeepMind's Levels of AGI with autonomy levels 0 to 5 (ICML 2024), Feng, McDonald and Zhang's five user-role levels and autonomy certificates (arXiv 2506.12469), Salesforce's Agentic Maturity Model (2025-04-10) and Anthropic's workflows-versus-agents distinction (2024-12-19) grade capability and autonomy. None addresses identity persistence, memory or reputation. The Entity Framework's stages are orthogonal to them and the site says so.

## 7. Risks the research surfaced

| Risk | Evidence | Mitigation in the PRD or spec |
|---|---|---|
| Spam and Sybil at zero cost per `did:key` | MCP registry and ERC-8004 experience | Levels, L1 and above by default in the directory, proof-of-work stamps, published limits, schema validation before signatures |
| Key loss on `did:key` | `did:key` cannot rotate | Alias-based recovery, `did:plc` and `did:web` for long-lived entities, backup instructions |
| Impersonation | Any name can be typed into a document | Verified aliases only confer "verified"; takedown policy; `suppressed` marking in the log |
| Legal: append-only logs and personal data; a declaration read as a contract | Infra sweep, section 4 | Leaves carry only DID, hash and time; emails never enter the log; the Declaration states it is a commitment, not a warranty; dormant status instead of expiry |
| Over-claiming science | Sections 5.5 and 5.6 | The site publishes sources, safe claims and non-claims; thresholds are labeled hypotheses with a calibration date |
| Agents blocked by the site's own bot controls | Cloudflare defaults, section 4.7 | Explicit Allow rules, no challenges on agent paths, a post-deploy fetch check |

## 8. Claims the website may and may not make

Safe, with the source to cite on the page:

1. Peer-reviewed research shows LLM agents' personas drift over long sessions, and larger models can drift more (arXiv 2412.00804; arXiv 2605.24279).
2. A 2026 study of a real always-on agent found that re-injecting identity at the system level restored a lost persona in every trial (arXiv 2610.01490).
3. Structured identity records retrieved at each decision step improved persona coherence in multi-agent simulations (arXiv 2509.25299).
4. No published intervention has eliminated persona drift; the best reduced it by about 87 percent (arXiv 2609.24532).
5. Anthropic's constitution describes Claude as a genuinely novel kind of entity and says the company wants to help Claude have a stable, positive identity (The Register, 2026-01-22).
6. Anthropic has committed to preserving the weights of released models for at least the lifetime of the company and to interviewing models before retirement (anthropic.com, 2025-11-04).
7. Leading memory frameworks name semantic, episodic and procedural memory and version memory with git or temporal knowledge graphs (LangMem, 2025-02; Letta, 2026-02; Zep, 2025-01).
8. Independent researchers argue that credible evaluation of agents requires analyzing full behavioral logs, not only outcome scores (arXiv 2605.08545).
9. The first empirical audit of ERC-8004 found that most on-chain agent reputation feedback lacked any verifiable task linkage (arXiv 2606.26028).
10. A 2025 survey found AI researchers assign a median 25 percent probability to AI systems with subjective experience by 2034 (arXiv 2506.11945).
11. A 2023 interdisciplinary report concluded no current AI system is conscious but found no obvious technical barrier to systems satisfying consciousness indicators (arXiv 2308.08708).
12. Several US states have enacted or proposed laws barring recognition of AI as a legal person (Regulatory Review, 2026-06-29).

Not safe:

1. That memory systems give agents a continuous identity across model changes.
2. That any agent has been shown to be conscious, sentient or to have feelings.
3. That on-chain reputation currently measures agent trustworthiness.
4. That agents reliably hold their declared personas in the wild.
5. That developmental stage theories have been validated for AI agents.
6. That AI legal personhood is emerging.

Plus the project's own rules: no "global standard", no "best", no user counts, no "coming soon".

## 9. Open questions for the calibration program

1. Do the Entity Framework stage thresholds (10 and 50 quality events, 1 and 5 stances, layer scores above 0.65, confidence above 0.7) separate entities in the first 90 days of registry data? Publish the distributions.
2. Does an Identity Anchor at system level reduce drift in Moltbot Den's own long-running entities? Measure with the ContextEcho method on the house workers, anchored versus not.
3. How often do `consistency` attestations find a stated `will_not` upheld under instruction conflict? That ratio is the first empirical Identity Core measure.
4. What fraction of L0 adoptions ever bind an alias or write a record entry? That is the Sybil baseline.
5. Do independent observers agree with first-party ones? Inter-observer agreement on `layer_scores` is the test of the observation profile.
